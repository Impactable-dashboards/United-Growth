WHITE SPACE  ·  SECTION 02

The market is a barbell

The necessity-retail field splits into two ends. At one end sit a handful of scaled sponsors with full demand engines that publish constantly. At the other sits a vast, fragmented long tail of private owner-operators with real assets and no marketing, the very owners Phillips Edison says it competes against for deals. United Growth sits in the middle: the forty-year record, the integrated platform and the development capability of the top tier, paired with the quiet digital footprint of the long tail.

Plotted on marketing presence against value-creation model, a clear space opens up. Among the private sponsors competing for the same investor capital, no one both builds and sources its own centers and is genuinely visible. That upper-right space is United Growth's to move into, on the strength of what it already does.

The structural read

The white space is not a message United Growth lacks. It is a position United Growth already holds, top-tier operating capability with a develop-and-source model, and has simply never claimed out loud. Every direct competitor for the same investor capital is either running an aggressive digital demand engine or distributing through the advisor and 1031 exchange channel. None pairs a forty-year integrated operating record with genuine ground-up development and off-market sourcing, told plainly. That combination is open.

WHITE SPACE  ·  SECTION 03

Four lanes United Growth can own

Each lane stands on what United Growth already does, maps to a specific buyer, and cites real proof. None of them require a marketing partner to be true.

LANE 01Create the asset, do not just buy it

Almost every capital-side competitor is an acquirer or aggregator. First National Realty Partners, ExchangeRight and Four Springs buy existing centers and net-lease assets. The story of a sponsor that both develops ground-up, build-to-suit properties and executes value-add acquisitions is essentially unclaimed in the investor conversation.

Why it matters: it puts investors earlier in the value-creation chain than a stabilized purchase, where more of the value is manufactured rather than bought at market. That is a genuinely different risk-and-return profile the category is not articulating.
Evidence: United Growth runs a purpose-built development platform, ground-up, build-to-suit and develop-to-core, alongside value-add acquisitions, with entitlements and pre-construction handled in-house (site). No competitor in the set pairs both engines in its messaging.
Serves: the investor who wants to be earlier than a stabilized-asset buyer, the developer or capital joint-venture partner, and the national net-lease tenant seeking a build-to-suit developer.
Opportunity size: High.
LANE 02Off-market sourcing as the visible edge

Everyone claims vertical integration. Almost no one builds a brand on where the deals actually come from. The off-market, relationship-sourced origination advantage is the least-marketed real moat in the category.

Why it matters: entry price sets the return. Anyone who knows real estate knows that sourcing off-market, away from auction pricing, is the most durable edge a sponsor can hold, and the hardest to copy, because it rests on decades of relationships.
Evidence: United Growth sources more than half of its deals off-market through direct relationships (site). Four Springs only hints at the small-cap niche; no competitor makes sourcing the headline. This is United Growth's to claim.
Serves: the sophisticated investor and family office that underwrites entry basis, and, on the other side, the brokers and sellers who supply off-market deals.
Opportunity size: High.
LANE 03Community impact as a returns thesis

Whitestone speaks to community, but frames it as a leasing tactic. No one in the set connects community impact to underwriting outcomes and investor returns in a single argument.

Why it matters: United Growth's own thesis is that centers which genuinely matter to their trade areas retain tenants longer, lease vacancies faster and hold value through cycles. That reframes community impact from a feel-good line into a driver of durable value, which is a differentiated investment argument rather than a brand mood.
Evidence: the thesis is already on United Growth's site. What it lacks is distributable proof, named centers with before-and-after trade-area outcomes. Whitestone's community framing confirms the theme resonates but stops short of the returns link.
Serves: values-aligned accredited investors, family offices and impact-curious allocators, plus municipalities and joint-venture partners evaluating a developer.
Opportunity size: Medium to high. Verification item: package two or three named centers with trade-area outcomes to move this from claim to proof.
LANE 04The deal-side audience nobody courts with content

The entire competitive marketing conversation points at investors. Almost no one runs a demand motion aimed at the people who source the deals: brokers, private sellers and 1031 exchange sellers.

Why it matters: this lane is wide open and it compounds Lane 02. A content and outreach motion aimed at brokers and owners feeds the off-market sourcing engine that already produces more than half of acquisitions, and it carries none of the securities-marketing constraints that govern the capital side.
Evidence: United Growth already sources off-market from these relationships (site). Formalizing a demand motion around them extends an existing strength rather than inventing a new one. No competitor in the set is visibly doing this.
Serves: commercial real estate brokers, private and family sellers of neighborhood and grocery-anchored centers, 1031 exchange sellers, and undercapitalized owners.
Opportunity size: High. The fastest, cleanest lane to activate, because it is unconstrained by investor-solicitation rules.
Open frontier, not a current advantage

Fee and structure transparency is the category's unhealed wound. Two of the direct competitors carry litigation or arbitration flags and undisclosed-fee criticism, and Delaware Statutory Trusts broadly draw high-fee complaints. It is tempting to call transparency United Growth's edge. It is not, yet. United Growth does not publish fees or a track record either, so this is a frontier open to everyone, United Growth included, rather than a lane the firm owns today. It belongs on the roadmap as a trust lever the firm could deliberately choose to own. Naming it honestly is itself the differentiator the category lacks.

One conversation a month becomes a month of premium content across every channel. The entry rhythm is three posts a week on leaders' profiles and one on the company page, stepping up to five and three at the top tier. Consistent and premium, never noise.

The weekly pillar mix

Each week balances the pillars so the feed teaches, proves and humanizes rather than just announcing, across both the leaders' profiles and the company page. A representative week leads with three angles:

Post 1

Insight or market read. Teaches or takes a position. The Market, or How It Works.

Post 2

Proof or place. A center, a milestone, a community story. Portfolio in Motion, or Community & Place.

Post 3

SECTION 04

The familiarity ladder

A private firm that does not want to solicit does not run a funnel with a call to action at the bottom. It builds familiarity in stages and lets interest arrive on its own. The measure at every stage is recognition and trust, never a captured lead.

1Discover

Encounters United Growth in the feed and recognizes a firm with a point of view. The Market and Community pillars catch the eye without asking for anything.

2Understand

Learns what United Growth actually does, and how it is different. Portfolio in Motion and How It Works make the model plain.

3Trust

Sees the people, the record and the values, and starts to regard the firm as credible. The Firm, Community and Place, and Where We Are build confidence.

4Reach out, on their own terms

Chooses to visit the site or make contact, unprompted. No push. Familiarity and credibility do the work.

The only call to action

At most, a quiet learn more at unitedgrowth.com. No lead forms, no invitations to invest, no urgency. The site visit is a natural next step, not an ask.

Never

  • Ask for investment or imply an offering.
  • Publish returns figures or performance claims.
  • Use urgency, hype or promotional language.
  • Run lead forms or hard calls to action.
  • Post simply to fill a calendar.
A note on posture

A brand-first, non-solicitous presence is not only the more premium choice, it also sits comfortably with the care a private fund understandably takes in how it communicates publicly. Building familiarity and authority, rather than advertising an offering, keeps the page firmly on the right side of that line while still doing real work for the firm.
